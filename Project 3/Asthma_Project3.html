Asthma_Project3(YP)

Introduction/Approach

For my portion of the group project, I will focus on adult asthma (age 16+) across New York City. I will use three datasets from the NYC Environment & Health Data Portal: adults with asthma, asthma-related emergency department visits, and asthma-related hospitalizations. These datasets will allow me to examine asthma burden from different perspectives and identify patterns across NYC communities.

I will clean and prepare the data in R, examine missing values and potential outliers, and perform exploratory analysis. I will compare asthma estimates across geographic areas and, where available, examine trends over time. The cleaned asthma data will then be incorporated into the group’s relational database and compared with the diabetes and high blood pressure findings.

Analysis Questions

Individual intake on Asthma prevalence across NYC:

How does asthma burden vary across NYC neighborhoods and boroughs, and which communities have the highest rates of asthma-related emergency department visits and hospitalizations?

Source: https://a816-dohbesp.nyc.gov/IndicatorPublic/data-explorer/asthma/

https://github.com/yeimiperez14/Data_607/tree/main/Project%203

Group Project connection question:

How do asthma, diabetes, and high blood pressure rates vary across New York City neighborhoods, and which communities experience the greatest health burdens?

Comparable Asthma Measures to be used:

Asthma prevalence How common asthma is
Asthma ED visit rate Indicates acute asthma burden
Asthma hospitalization rate Indicates more severe outcomes

Important note on dataset: The three asthma datasets may not use identical geographic boundaries or reporting periods. The NYC Environment & Health Data Portal provides indicators using different geographic definitions, including United Hospital Fund (UHF) neighborhoods and Neighborhood Tabulation Areas (NTAs), depending on the underlying data source. Reporting years may also differ across asthma prevalence, emergency department visits, and hospitalizations. These differences will be reviewed during data preparation and considered when comparing results across the three asthma measures.

# Function to find exactly one matching dataset
get_csv_url <- function(pattern) {
  
  matches <- csv_files %>%
    filter(str_detect(
      name, regex(pattern, ignore_case = TRUE)
    ))
  
  if (nrow(matches) != 1) {
    stop(
      "Expected one matching file for: ",
      pattern,
      ". Check the filenames."
    )
  }
  
  matches$download_url[[1]]
}

Findings

The analysis shows substantial differences in age-adjusted asthma prevalence across NYC UHF areas. East Harlem had the highest prevalence at 26.2%, followed by the Rockaways at 25.8% and Northeast Bronx at 25.0%. Several Bronx and Brooklyn UHF areas also appeared among the ten areas with the highest prevalence. These results identify geographic patterns in asthma prevalence but do not explain the causes of those differences.

Conclusion

Across the three datasets, asthma burden varied across New York City, with several Bronx communities consistently showing higher rates. In 2020, East Harlem had the highest age-adjusted asthma prevalence, while the Bronx had the highest asthma emergency department visit rate. In 2023, the highest hospitalization rates were concentrated in the Bronx, led by Hunts Point–Mott Haven. The recurrence of higher rates in certain communities suggests that asthma burden may be concentrated in specific areas rather than evenly distributed throughout the city. These findings also identify communities that may benefit from further analysis to better understand the factors associated with higher asthma burden. The findings suggest geographic disparities in asthma burden across NYC. However, because the datasets use different years and geographic levels, the results should be viewed as complementary patterns rather than direct comparisons.
